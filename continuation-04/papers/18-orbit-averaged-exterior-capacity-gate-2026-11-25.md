# Exterior bound-state capacity gate for orbit-averaged C22 transport

2026-11-25. Follow papers 16–17. Initial truncated halo ends at r_t, but physical binding extends to E=0. Use s=ψ−q², ψ=Qt/r−Qt/rt outside the initial halo, with dimensionless physical binding ε=s+Qt/rt>0. The orbit-energy density of states in the same normalization as previous papers is
g(s)=1/2 integral_0^{r_s} r² sqrt(ψ(r)−s) dr.
Indeed q²dq=−sqrt(ψ−s) ds/2. The dimensionless particle ledger is N=integral g(s)F(s)ds (normalization Ic Qt for initial profile). In a frozen exterior Kepler potential the allowed bound radius is r_s=Qt/ε. For 0<ε<Qt/rt, exact exterior contribution:
g_ext(s)=Qt³/(2 ε^(5/2)) integral_{ε rt/Qt}^1 x^(3/2)(1−x)^(1/2) dx.
As ε→0, complete beta B(5/2,3/2)=π/16, hence g_ext~π Qt³/(32 ε^(5/2)). The finite inner contribution cannot cancel this divergence. A finite-mass isotropic power-law tail F~ε^p requires p>3/2 (log marginal at p=3/2). A nonzero constant occupation all the way to physical escape gives infinite mass. This is a frozen potential asymptotic (also applies to finite-mass asymptotically Kepler potential), not a proof that transport dynamically selects such a tail.

Weakly bound Kepler orbital period in r/R, t V0/R units is 2π Qt/(2ε)^(3/2), independent of angular momentum. It diverges toward escape. Thus even when inner collisions are slow, a phase-mixed one-energy closure cannot automatically be used arbitrarily close to E=0 on finite evolution times. A finite-volume physical radius/time cutoff or explicit angular momentum/streaming exterior treatment is necessary; distinguish numerical cutoff from actual tidal environment. No artificial sink at s=0.

At each fixed r, pair collision quadrature uses exact local s1+s2=s3+s4 and same reversible U-U weight as paper 17. Integrate over radius with nonnegative weight r²dr and kinematically allowed velocity pairs. On fixed potential this gives number and energy conservation across all included bound+unbound states by event-wise pair ledger, independently of angular quadrature; when removing unbound particles track their actual E≥0 and number. Do not assume energy conservation for a changing potential without gravitational self-energy and adiabatic/streaming update. The outer g(s) singularity makes a constant-occupation final bin reaching ε=0 inadmissible; use weighted tail parameterization or finite-radius streaming, with mass and flux convergence.

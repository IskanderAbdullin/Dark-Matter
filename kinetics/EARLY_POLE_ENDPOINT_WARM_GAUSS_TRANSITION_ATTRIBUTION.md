# The Gauss3-to-Gauss4 pressure drop is principally a redistribution across warm cells

At each of the four common endpoints, reduce the evolved Gauss2, Gauss3 and Gauss4 states to the same original cells at the same archived W1 measurement mass. Decompose the change in each pressure contribution P=n h into the symmetric products Δn × mean(h) and mean(n) × Δh, where h is the pressure contribution per particle in the cell. All entries below are percentages of the earlier chart's full pressure at x=.05.

| Transition | Total | Changed cell counts | Changed within-cell pressure per particle |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| G2→G3 | +0.0826566% | +0.2498798% | −0.1672232% |
| G3→G4 | −0.1253897% | −0.1250164% | −0.0003734% |

Total number barely moves, but warm-cell number changes by +0.11978% and −0.03219% respectively. For G3→G4, the single parent q=[3.96368,5.72884] contributes −0.10401% of G3 pressure (about 83% of the net signed change); of that change −0.10701% is its changed count and +0.00300% is the per-particle compensation. This is a cell ledger of two different trajectories, not an intervention establishing the origin of changing counts.

As a cross-check, measure pressure using the positive evolved nodes directly in the 22 refined warm cells and the same native step convention in all other cells. The difference from the reconstructed pressure is no more than 1.31e-9 of full pressure across the 12 chart–epoch pairs. Both lie inside the previously measured conditional bounds. The Gauss pressure reversal is thus not generated by the selected warm reconstruction.

## Repeated fermion oscillations: no multiplying single-crossing yield
For a periodic prescribed background H(−φ)=S H(φ) S with S=diag(1,−1,1,−1). If U is the unitary half-period propagator, the full period is C=SUSU. At fixed p and periodic background the eigenvalues of C have modulus one, and C^N acts on the initially occupied negative-energy subspace; occupations stay within [0,1]. Unlike a bosonic Floquet instability, no positive real fermion occupation exponent can be inferred. Expansion, dephasing, scattering and available momentum volume can still allow additional production.
For λ=3.82587e−8, amplitude 1.1φcrit, two finite-time instantaneous vacuum choices (turning point φmax vs zero crossing) were compared on p≤.12 GeV and p≤.24 GeV grids. For 65,129,257 points, all cycle eigenvalue modulus defects <3e−8, operator norm unitarity defect <6e−8; max sampled occupation .9873 or below. The turning-point initial vacuum after 1 cycle gives light particle+antiparticle count 1.67186e−8,2.17933e−8,2.18344e−8 GeV³ at pmax=.12; the last two differ .19%. At 257 points energy fraction of the prescribed scalar background is 2.09e−8, at 129 points 2.08e−8. Doubling pmax at fixed spacing changes the turning-point 129-equivalent energy result <.1%. By contrast the zero-crossing instantaneous vacuum energy fraction increases from 7.86e−8 to 1.225e−7 on the same-spacing pmax extension, an explicit UV/initial-state warning. Higher cycles show strong phase oscillations and coarse-grid momentum nonconvergence (e.g. turning-point N=16 light number 4.739e−8 vs 1.825e−8 at 129 vs257). Do not use these late-cycle integrated counts as reliable yields. No cumulative cosmological number or physical energy transfer calculated; vacuum boundary, dephasing and scalar self-resonance must be solved.

## Scalar thermodynamic endpoints
For the normalized positive-quartic branch, define exact J=a^-3 J0 and energy ρ; in a genuinely dilute harmonic regime J approximates the number of scalar particles, not an exact conserved real-scalar charge. At a chosen visible T, consider (a) instantaneous complete scalar chemical equilibrium μ=0 at same energy, followed by immediate collisionless freeze; (b) an ideal critical μ=m gas plus zero-momentum reservoir, with total number fixed to J and energy available to excited kinetic degrees at most U=ρ−mJ, dropping residual positive interaction energy. Limit (b) is a diagnostic, NOT a physical BEC prediction; the interacting spectrum and residual interaction energy need separate treatment.

For dilute A, exact action gives ρ/(mJ)−1=3A²/8+O(A⁴); evaluate the difference by rationalizing sqrt(1+u)−1 to avoid catastrophic cancellation. Exact massive BE integrals and two scalar roots are locally checked by massless ρ/T⁴=π²/30, critical NR n→ζ(3/2)(mT/2π)^(3/2), and stable small-A action. In limit (b), the ideal critical distribution can store the same fixed count with an almost entirely zero-momentum component, but this says nothing about formation or lifetime. The exact energy inequality in an ideal particle description with conserved count J and nonnegative interaction energy, N(p≥p_cut)/J≤(ρ−mJ)/[J(sqrt(m²+p_cut²)−m)], requires no thermal assumption. At the chosen q_cut=1.14255e−6 eV it yields ≤2.30551e−6 and ≤1.19589e−4 for the two dilute examples. This bound fails if number-changing frees rest energy, precisely the distinction from (a). The code also explores earlier quartic epochs as *formal* diagnostics; do not identify J with a particle count there.

# A per-cell number-and-energy closure does not cure thin-face width sensitivity

The [thin-face live comparison](INDEPENDENT-EARLY-POLE-BOUNDED-FACE-LONG-WIDTH-2026-11-28.md) remained strongly dependent on face width despite independently refined time steps. Try a constrained redistribution within each original three-subcell charged bin after each bounded midpoint advance, with no change to the scalar state. Preserve the bin's number and first energy moment while selecting the Fermi entropy maximum along the remaining one-dimensional feasible segment.

In count variables, the redistribution direction is proportional to (E₁−E₂, E₂−E₀, E₀−E₁); its dot products with both unity and centroid energy vanish. The Pauli bounds constrain the available segment. A bracketed entropy derivative finds its maximum; after calculation the one scalar redistribution parameter is moved towards the original feasible state if floating-point rounding hits a bound. The final occupations are not clipped. This is an imposed closure, not a microscopic collision term.

Both original archives again advance by Δlog(a)=5e−5 using 128 and 256 steps; all occupation bounds and independently checked energy and number budgets hold. But shrinking the face fraction from 1/4096 to 1/32768 yields only .307 (fine) and .157 (third) of the original scalar increment, even with the constrained remap. In the 256-step cases, redistribution inside the occupied charge bins moves .78 to 2.00 times the magnitude of the net scalar increment (an absolute-movement diagnostic, not a net source). It also increases entropy artificially, so its approximate effect is not negligible. Preserve this as a failed closure candidate rather than tuning it to the original trajectory.

# Enforcing a zero divergence is not a prescription for the regulated current

The [bare Ward test](INDEPENDENT-FINITE-MASS-QED-NONNULL-PHOTON-WARD-POLES-2026-11-27.md) omits finite widths. Reuse the [archived coalescence geometry](INDEPENDENT-FINITE-MASS-QED-COALESCING-HISTOGRAM-EDGE-2026-11-28.md) to test the divergence of the arbitrary width insertions used there. Let S be the scalar spinor bilinear. For the current with bare numerators and denominators replaced by `D_a+i ε_a`, the exact contraction is
```text
K·J = S [D1/(D1+i ε1) − D2/(D2+i ε2)].
```
This follows directly from the mass-shell Dirac equations, which leave +D1 and −D2 in the two numerator contractions. At exact coalescence D2 = −D1 the choice ε2 = −ε1 makes this expression vanish for every ε1. Common-sign widths generally violate this identity even though the bare current obeys it. This algebra does not identify the physical contour signs or an on-shell damping rate.

For any reference r with K·r≠0 one can also set `J_r = J − r (K·J)/(K·r)`. The difference between two such completions is orthogonal to K and is therefore not determined by the Ward condition. In 48 archived kinematic fixtures and two imaginary orientations, the explicit spinor contraction reproduces the above identity to 6.22e−7 relative to ε|S| for the **scaled** current εJ. All completions have residual divergence below 1.13e−14 relative to the norm of that current. Despite this, the squared response of a single spatial transverse polarization varies by up to 1.16e−4 absolute (4.01e−4 relative to the larger result); for a plasma-longitudinal polarization the respective maximum differences are 1.92e−4 absolute and 3.74e−4 relative. The reported squared responses use a common local transverse-residue norm; they do not include spectral or occupation weights.

Projection parallel to K preserves either physical polarization because its polarization vector is orthogonal to K. Projection parallel to the bath time direction preserves the spatial transverse polarization but can change the longitudinal one. Tilting the reference spatially gives an explicitly different spatial transverse current with the same small Ward residual. These reference vectors are counterexamples to uniqueness, not candidates for the true dressed vertex. As a comparison, [Arnold, Moore and Yaffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0109064) find that multiple scattering modifies the leading photon-emission result in a weakly coupled ultrarelativistic plasma. That result motivates testing the dressing rather than just projecting the current here; their approximations and equation are not imported into this massive scalar channel.

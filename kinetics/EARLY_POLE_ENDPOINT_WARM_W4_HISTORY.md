Retain exactly the same [clock and restart conventions](EARLY_POLE_ENDPOINT_WARM_GAUSS_HISTORY.md) as the other independently evolved warm charts. At each stage the two post-restart clock levels, paired number, Pauli, and continuity tests pass. The saved states include the exact fine-time occupations at each clock boundary; all integrations below use each spectrum at the same W1 reference mass, not each chart's own fitted mass.

At x=.05, W4 pressure is **7.92178% lower** than W1, while its total scalar number differs by **−3.9440e−7 relative**. The independent warm Gauss2 pressure is **0.57250% lower** than W4. The Gauss2-versus-W4 pressure difference ranges from −0.596% to −0.572% on the four sampled endpoints; the separate activated warm grid and finer number/time checks do not remove this numerical spread. W4 has 211 scalar nodes while Gauss2 has 167; lower node count alone does not rank the algorithms. Neither chart has a certified momentum-space limit.

In the [fixed-shape stress sweep](EARLY_POLE_ENDPOINT_FROZEN_STRESS_SWEEP.md) the four-way receipt accepts the independently evolved W4 state as an additional input. At formal weight (M=10), W4 pressure and pseudo-pressure are lower than W1 by 11.560% and 18.850%, and warm Gauss2 by 12.090% and 19.049%. At (M=100), Gauss2 still gives a lower pressure than W4 but a **higher** pseudo-pressure: relative to W1 the latter changes by −21.769% versus W4's −21.881%. Thus the ordering depends on the observable and weight. It is not a physical time or mass prediction.

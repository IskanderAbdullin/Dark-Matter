# Why the frozen cold-band matrix is sensitive to a small dispersion shift

The [cold/shoulder comparison](INDEPENDENT-FINITE-MASS-QED-FIXED-POLE-SCALAR-SHOULDER-2026-11-28.md) reproduced the same regional sign contrast on both archives, but the cold forward coefficient differs by about 7.6. This test isolates the kinematic parts of that discrepancy at fixed k=.0015 and five cold q. It is not an integrated attribution of the band difference.

Evaluate all eight combinations of the charged occupation histograms, scalar pole mass and photon pole (frequency and residue taken together). The charged masses are the same on both archives. These are controlled substitutions in the same angular/radial integrals; only the original two endpoints use mutually consistent archive inputs. For each raw affine kernel A, B and D, decompose the log change from the all-third to all-fine result by averaging the marginal change over all six ordering choices. These changes add exactly on the log scale, and exponentiating them gives multiplicative factors. None includes the scalar histogram factor; the two possible scalar weights are reported separately.

At q=3.5e−6 and cosine zero, the parent-energy onset is 18.35145 in the fine fixture and 20.07942 in the third. Increasing the magnitude of the negative transfer energy closes much of the radial aperture before the lower-energy charged histogram can contribute. A small difference of the scalar pole mass changes this rare tail threshold appreciably. Across the five q the fine/third forward-kernel ratio before scalar weights is 6.06–7.62. Its factors are 4.38–5.29 (scalar mass), 1.37–1.43 (photon pole), and only 1.0079–1.0084 (substituting the charged histograms with dispersion fixed). For the inverse kernel the factors are 4.95–6.07, 1.41–1.48, and 1.046–1.050. All 40 paired angular integrals converge to 3.36e−10 or better by the measured maximum componentwise change.

The actual cold scalar occupations vary separately: the ratio at these five sample points ranges from .286 to 5.70, and the archived scalar edges differ. The small effect of swapping only the charged histograms does not show that the fitted dispersion or the final scalar tail is converged. The pointwise counterfactuals identify a dominant sensitivity to the dispersion; a shared-q-mesh integration is still needed to attribute the full weighted difference.

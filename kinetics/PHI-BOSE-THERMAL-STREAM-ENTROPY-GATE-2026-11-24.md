This is an independent diagnostic of the structure-formation requirement in GOAL, not a fit or a physical prediction of the Yukawa portal. Assume scalar m=86 eV, g=1, all dark matter at Omega h²=.120, collisionless relativistic-decoupling BE spectrum f(q)=(exp(q/T0)-1)^-1, no condensate for the first calculation, a0=1 and comoving q. T0 is fixed by physical number, not selected to make the spectrum artificially cold. Compare an all-DM thermal FD g=2 relic of mass 5.7 keV. Irsic et al., [Phys. Rev. ## 2. Cold-spectrum target and huge occupation without a BEC claim
If an arbitrary primordial 86-eV collisionless PSD is required to match the NR rms q/m of the 5.7-keV reference as a chosen engineering diagnostic (not necessary/sufficient for the actual Ly-alpha likelihood), then q_rms <=1.1425535848167361e-6 eV. Ordinary all-DM BE has q/m rms 274.627455 times larger. Markov inequality on q² implies at least half the particles lie inside q_ball=sqrt(2)*q_target. With g=1 and physical abundance, the average occupation over this ball is >=(n0/2)*6*pi²/q_ball³=792913.97. This is a high-occupation requirement, **not** evidence of delta-function condensation or proof of any burst production. If a pure q=0 population exists plus a zero-mu BE tail of fraction f, its overall q/m rms scales as f^(5/6); matching the reference rms gives f<=.001184424 only under this specific PSD and moment-matching diagnostic, NOT an observational constraint on mixed DM.

## 3. Rigorous ideal-gas entropy upper bound at fixed cold second moment
This avoids assuming the shape of the cold PSD. For homogeneous primordial distributions extrapolated in comoving momentum to a0=1, let M2=int d³q/(2*pi)^3 q² f = n*q_rms². The dimensionless Gibbs variational inequality applied to the trial single-particle cost q² gives, for any beta>0,
    s <= beta*M2 + A*beta^(-3/2), A=zeta(5/2)/(8*pi^(3/2)).
Indeed the Bose mode entropy h(f)=(1+f)ln(1+f)-f ln f is maximized after subtracting beta*q²*f by f=1/(exp(beta*q²)-1); integrating the mode log-partition gives A. There is no physical thermal temperature assumption in this argument. A macroscopically occupied zero mode contributes zero extensive entropy in the ideal-gas thermodynamic limit. Optimize beta=(3A/(2*M2))^(2/5), yielding
    s <= (5/2)*A*beta^(-3/2).
Using all-DM n0 as an upper bound on scalar count and the chosen cold moment target, M2<=1.4747993835e-25 eV^5, beta=2.4800019764e9 eV^-2, and s_phi<=0.07933673314 cm^-3. The mathematical maximizing excited BE(q²) distribution contains only .06179927 cm^-3, so if one additionally imposes the all-DM count, saturation would require a zero-entropy remainder .99579697 by number. **This is a mathematical extremizer, not a predicted condensate fraction.**

Even allowing stable heavy fermions to carry entropy, if they contribute <=1% of observed dark energy and are ideal 20/100MeV Dirac fields, Gibbs energy inequality at trial T=20MeV/90 bounds s_H <= (rho_H+P_MB(T))/T=5.7161590e-5 cm^-3, using the analytic MB pressure upper bound P_FD<=g*m²*T²*K2(m/T)/(2*pi²). FD log-partition quadrature independently checks the upper bound. Thus s_total<=.0793938947 cm^-3, compared with inherited initial common-temperature isolated benchmark s_D0=(7.991670795/61.75)*2891.2=374.18 cm^-3. At most 0.000212181907 (0.0212182%) of that benchmark entropy can be carried by such a cold final primordial distribution; >99.97878% entropy ratio reduction would be necessary. This is conditional on the chosen rms diagnostic, scalar rest mass dominance/<=1% heavy energy, ideal quasiparticle entropy, no other entropy carriers, conserved visible entropy normalization, and homogeneous primordial kinetic PSD rather than halo gravitational velocities. It is **not** a universal Bose DM no-go; colder initial conditions, entropy export, other degrees or a PSD with a different actual transfer-function bound can change the answer.

## Finite daughter masses and thermal cell cuts

The fixed-bath reference derived the two finite-mass number-rate measure from isotropic parent momentum and angle: d3p times M/E times dcos/2 changes to M/eta times dE_phi dE_f (eta=2p*/M), holding rest width fixed. It compared its numerical finite-volume cut-cell number and both physical daughter-energy moments against independent parent-angle integration. All three vacuum weights agree within 3e-11 relative in ten grid/parameter choices. The number and energy normalization were tested independently of the evolved solver. The deliberately wrong preliminary Fermi log-difference denominator gave 4% error on the coarse lattice; retaining the correct upper-endpoint denominator removed it.

At M/T=.1 and daughter/parent mass ratios (.08,.18), the L1 false equilibrium scalar flux (normalized by vacuum rate) of the integrated constant-occupation closure fell from 0.217 to 0.00377 on 32 to 256 cells; the artificial balanced-center closure cancels the point-nodal thermal net source exactly but shifts the initial vacuum rate by about 0.151% even at 256 cells. At tau=1e-4, relative physical-versus-shell energy source defect was 6.36% on 32 cells and 0.0996% on 256. Thus conserved daughter numbers and a zero algebraic equilibrium test do not settle physical energy. Source support with forbidden cell centroids is measured, not deleted. The additional M/T=.005 and 86-eV/20-MeV benchmark-ratio controls are not actual cosmological parameters or physical yields: bath and width are imposed, time is short, and qmin truncates the IR.

The cell-cut test evaluated FD/BE occupations on the true energies by inferring local chemical potentials from exact number-weighted shell means at common temperature. It preserved chemical equilibrium in all 12 tested grid/offset combinations; affinity residual below 1.4e-16. At equilibrium, replacing that reconstruction by piecewise constant shell occupations gave 2.37–10.59% absolute spurious reaction traffic divided by gross traffic, depending on mass and resolution. A shifted parent chemical potential verified the exp(0.2) forward/reverse ratio; summed physical energy closed. Neither calculation implements evolving reconstructed shell numbers, dynamic SM entropy, or composition-resolved FRW and neither is CLASS-ready.


## Number-only shape diagnostics

A reference shape inversion handles exactly empty cells, exactly filled FD cells, and an artificial continuation of BE cells which cannot be fit thermally at the specified temperature: for above-capacity BE, scale a saturated shape by N/N_cap (do not infer a condensate). Forty low-to-high-momentum checks preserve cell number within 1.80e-11 relative. For a lowest shell 0<q<Q much narrower than the mass, a flat occupation and a rescaled saturated occupation carry the same number but different kinetic energy. The leading mean kinetic energies are 3Q^2/(10m) and Q^2/(6m) respectively, hence the ratio 5/9; the independent energies integrated from the represented shapes give 0.555555559. Moment-matching number alone does not justify a chosen above-capacity distribution or any rates computed from it. Carry number and energy closure diagnostics separately in the next finite-volume stage, test shape sensitivity, and never relabel the artificial shape as physical BEC.

## Two-moment admissibility, closure and local entropy

1. Two finite daughters, true cut-cell event energies, fixed bath and short physical-time control `t Gamma=1e-4`: the number-based thermal reconstruction reached scalar equilibrium projected residual below 1.7e-14 relative to vacuum rate. Constant occupancy produced a 25.65%, 6.99%, 1.79% false equilibrium projected source on 24, 48 and 96 shells at M/T=.1. The independently integrated event energy ledger closed, but the energy of the represented shell shape did not: relative gap 5.27%, 1.36%, 0.342% on those grids. Tightening temporal tolerance alone on 24 cells left the gap unchanged to the displayed precision. Above-capacity alternatives were compared only as diagnostics. Early M/T=.005 cases and independent cut-quadrature refinement retained. Initial overly relative number guard failed at near-cancellation of two large thermal populations; absolute floating-point error based on populated cells is now explicit.
2. Exact Pauli realizability bounds for number plus kinetic energy: at fixed number, fill the lowest or highest momentum part of a shell up to Pauli capacity. Independent linear programming on 521 and 4096 microcells reproduced the bounds with max relative discrepancy 4.89e-4 and 2.93e-5 respectively. At 80% fill in the cold reference shell, the maximum accessible fermionic kinetic energy is only 69.87% of the unbounded case at the same number. Initial coarse-grid tolerance was not met; objective normalization is essential for near-rest energies.
3. Interior quantum FD moment inversion: local logistic shape `f=expit(alpha-beta*s)` with normalized kinetic energy `s`, beta possibly negative for a local shape only. At fixed number, derivative of the energy moment with beta is minus a nonnegative weighted variance. Recovery agreed with independent quadrature within 5e-14 relative in 24 tests and rejected a deliberately impossible pair. Endpoints still need explicit solver policies.
4. BE moment ambiguity: two positive closures matched the same number and kinetic energy, but a physical on-shell test with thermal fermions found reactions differing by up to 6.61% (quadrature drift 2.1e-12); this is a conditional, coarse-cell sensitivity, not an IR/BEC prediction. Neither closure establishes a physical distribution merely because two moments agree.
5. Fixed-temperature H-theorem control within the thermal BE cell capacity: reconstructing from shell numbers gives event-level `(G-L) log(G/L) >= 0`. The discrete daughter free-energy derivative equals minus this production when the parent bath has zero chemical potential. Mixed gain/loss cases at both masses and 24/48 cells matched an independent finite difference within 2.31e-6 relative. This says nothing about entropy of the artificial above-capacity continuation or a self-consistent expanding bath.


6. Regular local BE two-moment feasibility at the lowest cell. At fixed number, energy decreases monotonically with positive slope beta while the normal thermal amplitude exists; the upper allowed beta is set by the integrated saturation capacity at zero additive exponent. Adaptive integration in q and an independent change to square-root kinetic energy agreed to better than 1e-9 relative across near-rest and semirelativistic shells. For high occupation in the near-rest shell, the smallest mean regular-thermal kinetic fraction tends to 1/3; the numerical value was 0.333332 at mean occupation 1e5. This is a domain limit of the assumed regular shape, not evidence for a real condensate.
7. True-event single-step number and kinetic-moment update. In eight forward/reverse cut-quadrature tests, the proposed accepted step preserved both number transfers and the event energy with the parent debit; occupancy and shell energy were checked without clipping. The first infeasible moment boundary was energy, not number, in all eight (difference from the number-only maximum was small, 3.3e-7 to 5.3e-4 relative). This verifies that a number-capacity check alone is insufficient even before time integration. The gate is a single proposed state step, not a full scheme or an FRW run. The continuous admissible set is convex: at fixed FD number, the minimum energy is convex and the maximum concave; the Bose bounds are linear. Use this with simultaneous positive timestep rejection for actual multistep trials.


## Paired evolution, collisionless bounds and short prescribed expansion

1. Implemented simultaneous true-event two-moment evolution with bound-checked Heun steps. The daughter difference and total energy including the parent reservoir debit drifted by at most 3.34e-16 in the tested units. Three BE reconstructions use a flat positive prior, a thermal positive prior, and a normal entropy-shape inversion. The flat prior fails the exact thermal projected source test (up to 3.45e-3 of vacuum source), despite matching both moments. Both thermal and regular BE reconstructions passed within 7.4e-14. Regular BE can reject a moment pair outside its resolved normal-shape domain; the separate 28-case inversion benchmark agreed within 4.63e-12 with independent quadrature and refused the intentionally impossible cold/high-population control without inferring BEC. For three regular BE runs the independently formed positive event entropy production agreed with the moment derivative including the bath entropy to 1.48e-16 relative. Shape/space/time sensitivity remains separately auditable; this is not a physical rate-complete solver.
2. At fixed comoving cell number and kinetic moment, pressure has exact support-only bounds from the concavity of E - m^2/E: the chord gives a lower bound and Jensen gives an upper bound. Collisionless expansion maps each initial E to sqrt(E^2 + m_new^2 - m_old^2), convex for expansion; Jensen/chord gives independent lower/upper bounds on the transported kinetic moment. The numerics use stable mass-subtracted energies. Across two manufactured distributions and scale factors from 1 to 10000, the widest pressure ranges were 6.51%/6.78% on 8 shells and 0.0337%/0.0565% on 128; the widest propagated kinetic ranges were 65.24%/64.53% and 0.297%/0.298% respectively. The initial 64-shell goal of a >33x narrower pressure range failed for the fermion (observed about 30.2x), so 128 shells were checked. The exact pressures and ballistic energies stayed within the analytic bounds throughout.
3. Short prescribed FRW window, delta log a=.02; collision time tau=.001 externally specified. With actual moment states and fixed comoving momentum grid, redshift was separately audited by integrating the trace over log a independently of the endpoint energy difference. Across tested meshes/ratios and methods, daughter-number difference stayed within 6e-17 absolute, and total energy plus reservoir debit minus pressure-work had residual below 5e-15. Symmetric splitting's L1 state difference decreased from 5.23e-9 at 3 vs 6 steps to 1.39e-9 at 6 vs 12; Lie's corresponding values were 1.50e-7 and 7.37e-8. Pure streaming matched direct one-shot transport exactly for one interval; repeated projection adds small shape drift (2.34e-11 L1 at six steps for delta log a=.5). The fixed parent reservoir is not an SM temperature evolution or a conserved standalone dark sector.

These controls prescribe the parent reservoir and expansion. They are not a dynamical thermal-bath model. Separate physical microphysics and momentum/time/IR convergence are necessary before any late-spectrum claim.

## Explicit gauge-covariant illustrative mediator

One possible consistent vector current before electroweak
breaking uses the anomaly-free family-lepton gauge
combination U(1)_(L_e−L_μ):

    L_int = Z'_μ [
      gχ qχ χ̄γ^μχ
      +g' (L̄_e γ^μ L_e + ē_R γ^μ e_R
             − L̄_μ γ^μ L_μ − μ̄_R γ^μ μ_R) ].

Here L_e=(ν_eL,e_L)^T and L_μ=(ν_μL,μ_L)^T
are electroweak doublets; χ is a **vectorlike SM-singlet
Dirac fermion**. A gauge breaking sector is needed to
produce a mass mZ'. Integrating out a sufficiently heavy
Z' produces approximately G=gχ qχ g'/mZ'² and
a dark vector current coupled to both e−/e+ and
ν_e,ν_μ with opposite muon-family charge signs.
The signs square out of elastic |M|². This U(1)
candidate can be anomaly free; it is **not** shown
to reproduce the actual project's χ1/χ2/φ field
content, scalar decay coupling or cosmological history.
Kinetic mixing, loop processes, mediator mass, high-T
propagator, gauge breaking and experimental constraints
must be evaluated separately.

A vectorlike e_L+e_R coupling cannot silently
ignore the ν_eL component of its SU(2)L doublet.
A purely e_R or additional-spectator completion is
another model with different anomaly/electroweak
requirements, not the same tested vectorlike-electron
benchmark.

The contact approximation is **only conditional**
where |t|≪mZ'² and temperatures below mediator
and electroweak scales; the real propagator is
gχ qχ g'/(mZ'²−t) and may change the momentum
kernel, rates, thermal effects and constraints.
Our arbitrary G=.001 me⁻² is a *numerical
normalization*, not a measured or viable physical
coupling. The ratio of neutrino and electron heat
cancels the common G² in this simplified EFT.

## Independently checked massless chiral trace

For a massless active left-handed ν,
the single-helicity current tensor is

    L_L^{μν}=Tr[k̸' γ^μ P_L k̸ γ^ν P_L],
    P_L=(1−γ5)/2,

while for spin-averaged Dirac-vector m=0 it is

    L_V^{μν}=(1/2)Tr[k̸' γ^μ k̸ γ^ν].

Their **symmetric** components are equal;
the left-current axial antisymmetric ε^{μνρσ}
part vanishes on contraction with the symmetric
Dirac vector-χ tensor. Thus for *one physical
active neutrino helicity* the spin-summed
squared amplitude per occupied incoming degree
equals the massless Dirac-vector average, **but**
one must count ν and antineutrino degrees correctly
rather than assign four spin states to a single ν.

We independently contract actual complex 4×4 Dirac
γμ/γ5 matrices at Mχ/me=.1,p*=.5me and
CM cosθ*=−.8,−.2,+.3,+.9.
The explicit chiral ν trace, independent
Dirac-vector lepton trace and analytic invariant
polynomial

    |M|²_avg/G² = 2[(s−Mχ²)²+
                        (u−Mχ²)²+2Mχ² t]

agree to machine precision (≤~5×10⁻¹⁶
relative on these points). This is conditional on
**Dirac vector χ**; an axial χ current requires a
different parity-odd interference calculation.

At T≪mμ, the two active channels
ν_e,ν̄_e,ν_μ,ν̄_μ have total degeneracy
gν=4 (one helicity each). Electrons and positrons
also total ge=4 (two spin states each). Opposite
L_e−L_μ charges cause no elastic cancellations
in |M|². Real ν flavor kinetics, neutrino
masses/mixing and distributions are not solved.

## More serious counterexample: distinct Tν and Tγ

After photon reheating, the ideal instantaneous
e±-annihilation limit often used for illustration
is Tν/Tγ=(4/11)^{1/3}=0.7137658555,
*not* a result from this project or an exact
finite-temperature SM ν-decoupling calculation.
Set Tγ=Te=.1me, Tχ=.9Tγ,
Tν=(4/11)^{1/3} Tγ, same conditional
G and 5D Pauli amplitudes. At n44:

    Qχ|e± = +1.50804492×10⁻²⁰ me⁵,
    Qχ|2ν = −7.72434429×10⁻¹⁸ me⁵,
    Qν/Qe ≈ −512.20916.

**The channels now exchange heat in opposite
directions.** The electrons are warmer than χ,
but the neutrinos are colder, so they extract
energy from χ; under equal contact coupling,
the ν contribution dominates and the total
net heat is negative. At artificially equal
Tν=Te, both channels heat χ and Qν/Qe≈1086.
This is a quantitative **counterexample**
to treating the SM as one temperature / one
electron BGK bath; not a prediction of the
physical cosmic heat sign. A real calculation
must evolve e±,γ,ν entropy and weak decoupling
(including possible Z' feedback) before drawing
cosmological conclusions.
